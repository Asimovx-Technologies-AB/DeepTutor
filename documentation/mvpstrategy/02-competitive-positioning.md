# 02 — Competitive Positioning

## Market reality

The product is entering an established category. Most visible capabilities can already be obtained from existing products.

### Direct and adjacent alternatives

| Alternative | Relevant capability | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Alice | Uploads, knowledge breakdown, study plans, AI tutoring, quizzes, flashcards, mock exams, progress | The material-to-plan-to-exam workflow already exists, including in a Swedish company. |
| Studi/Binogi | Lgr22-related grades 7–9 theoretical content, Biology, multilingual videos and quizzes, teacher statistics | Swedish curriculum alignment and multilingual Biology are not unique. |
| ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, NotebookLM | Upload, explanations, quizzes, summaries, planning, multilingual interaction | Individual AI features will continue to become cheaper and better. |
| MyStudent.ai | Nordic curriculum, AI tutor, homework, plans, evaluation, parent and teacher workflows | The broader Nordic K–12 concept already exists outside Sweden. |
| StarSpark and similar K–12 tutors | Adaptive learning, standards alignment, mastery tracking, parent portal and reports | Parent visibility and mastery dashboards are established features. |
| Human tutors | Trust, contextual judgment, motivation, and accountability | The product must offer sufficient structure and evidence at a materially lower price. |

## What is commoditised

The following must be treated as required infrastructure, not differentiation:

- PDF and document upload;
- OCR and chunking;
- vector retrieval and GraphRAG;
- document-grounded chat;
- summaries and smart notes;
- flashcards and quiz generation;
- generic study-plan generation;
- multilingual explanations;
- mock examination generation;
- simple progress charts.

## Candidate competitive wedge

The narrow unproven opportunity is the complete combination of:

1. Swedish parent as purchaser and controller.
2. Child-safe learning environment.
3. The actual material and assessment instructions supplied by the child's school.
4. Lgr22 concept mapping.
5. Diagnostic assessment before teaching.
6. Day-by-day preparation until a known test date.
7. Adaptation based on demonstrated learning.
8. Swedish subject terminology combined with an alternative explanation language.
9. Controlled pre/post evidence.
10. Concise parent visibility without requiring the parent to understand the subject.

No individual item is defensible. The product must win through the completeness and usability of the workflow, trusted execution, local distribution, and evidence of learning.

## Positioning

### Category

Parent-managed test preparation for Swedish lower-secondary students.

### Primary message

> Stop guessing whether your child is prepared for the test.

### Product explanation

> Upload the material from school. The programme identifies the child's gaps, creates a daily plan, guides each session, and shows what improved before test day.

### Supporting message

> Swedish school terminology, explanations the family can understand, and progress parents can see.

## Messaging to avoid

- “Chat with your textbook.”
- “Powered by GraphRAG.”
- “Multi-agent AI education.”
- “Better than ChatGPT.”
- “Guaranteed grades.”
- “AI that does homework.”
- “A complete tutor for every subject and every child.”

## Sustainable advantage, if earned

Potential defensibility must be built from:

- reviewed Swedish curriculum and misconception models;
- longitudinal concept-level learning evidence;
- validated assessment and pedagogical workflows;
- trust and recognition among Swedish parents;
- relationships with teachers, tutoring providers, and schools;
- child-safety and privacy maturity;
- superior local onboarding and support;
- proven acquisition and retention channels;
- the ability to add a curriculum unit efficiently and safely.

## Competitive test

Before scaling, the same Swedish Biology test material must be evaluated in:

- the current product;
- Alice;
- ChatGPT;
- Claude;
- Gemini or NotebookLM;
- Studi/Binogi when access permits.

Evaluate material understanding, Swedish terminology, diagnostic quality, plan usefulness, misconception detection, learning interaction, parent visibility, safety, latency, cost, and outcome improvement.

The MVP should continue only if the complete experience is materially more useful to the target parent than assembling the same workflow with an existing general tool.
